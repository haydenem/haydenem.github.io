Research

My research focuses on air-sea coupled processes that contribute to climate variability and predictability, with a particular focus on spatial and temporal scale-interactions.

Bering Sea Thermal Variability Air–Sea Heat Exchange Cross-Timescale Air–Sea Coupling

Bering Sea Thermal Variability

The Bering Sea has undergone pronounced temperature swings over recent decades. My work characterizes the atmospheric circulation processes that drive this variability, separating the roles of large-scale circulation, surface fluxes, and ocean response across the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

Hayden & O'Neill (2024), Journal of Climate

Air–Sea Heat Exchange

Anomalies in atmospheric circulation reorganize the exchange of heat between the ocean and the atmosphere. I quantify how circulation variability drives anomalies in Bering Sea air–sea heat fluxes, linking synoptic-scale atmospheric patterns to the surface energy budget.

Hayden, O'Neill & Zippel (2024), Journal of Climate

Cross-Timescale Air–Sea Coupling

Air–sea interactions couple processes acting on very different timescales. Using bispectral statistics, I identify cross-timescale coupling in the Bering Sea to understand how variability at one timescale modulates exchange at another.

Hayden, Zippel, Wettstein & O'Neill, (2026), Journal of Climate
