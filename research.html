Cross-Timescale Air–Sea Coupling

The atmospheric phenomena that regulate Bering Sea upper ocean temperature through their modulation of surface turbulent heat flux variability have not been definitively identified. I identified the strong coupling between the surface turbulent hear fluxes and anomalies large-scale meridional heat and moisture advection by the atmosphere, and showed that the canonical view that synoptic-scale variability drives Bering Sea turbulent heat flux anomalies is incomplete. Though a novel application of bispectral statistics, I showed new evidence of cross-frequency interactions in the coupled system of the Bering Sea, which limits the candidate mechanisms driving Bering Sea thermal variability through their modulation of surface turbulent heat exchange to those with a strong intraseasonal component and/or cross-frequency interactions.

Hayden, Zippel, Wettstein & O'Neill, (2026), Journal of Climate

The Role of Atmospheric Circulation in Bering Sea Thermal Variability

The dominant role of atmospheric variability on weekly to seasonal timescales in forcing Pacific basin-scale sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies is generally well-described. However, the role of the atmosphere in relatively small-scale SST and upper ocean temperature variability in the Bering Sea is less clear. I characterized the drivers of anomalous surface forcing over the Bering Sea by deriving a decomposition of the surface turbulent heat fluxes and showing that they are primarily driven by anomalies in near-surface temperature and specific humidity. Further, I showed that the air-mass anomalies that dominate the surface flux variability in the Bering Sea are cyclic functions of wind direction. These results indicate that variability in atmospheric circulation is the dominant driver of regional upper ocean temperature.

Hayden, O'Neill & Zippel (2024), Journal of Climate

Drivers of Recent Bering Sea Warming

In recent years, the Bering Sea, a subpolar marginal sea of the North Pacific, has experienced record warm ocean temperature anomalies and an increase in both air-sea heat flux variability and marine heatwaves. I characterized the role of surface forcing and ocean dynamical processes on regional mixed layer temperature tendency over the late 20th and early 21st centuries. I showed that surface forcing dominated the ocean temperature tendency, suggesting the critical role of air-sea coupling in driving the amplified upper ocean temperature variability.

Hayden & O'Neill (2024), Journal of Climate
